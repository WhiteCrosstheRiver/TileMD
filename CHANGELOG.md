# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- FP32 fused kick-drift step. The steps are queued and the host syncs once at the end. `tilemd_fp32_check` compares five steps at one thread per atom with the host walk. That binary is compiled and has not been launched.
- Host FP32 neighbor walk. On a displaced 864-atom fcc it passes T1-v2 against the double oracle. A perfect lattice does not: its net force is roundoff, so the force ratio is noise. Ten kick-drift steps stay inside the box.
- FP32 LJ pair function and cell-local minimum image. On boxes of length 32 and 33.5 the shadow delta matches the double minimum image. The fused atom-list force kernel builds as `tilemd_fp32_check` and is not the engine default yet.
- Speed-phase baseline harness. `tilemd_bench` times `atom`, `fused-tile`, and `atom-ref` with the section 8 step counts. `bench/scripts/run_bench.py` writes the JSON and `reports/SPEED.md`.
- Physical LJ fixtures. `boundary_500`, `dense_2000`, `twotype_2000`, and `dilute_1000` now keep every pair at least `0.85σ` apart. The old near-contact copies are in `tests/fixtures/stress/`.
- T1-v2 and T4-v2 checks. The recorded double-path drift passes T4-v2. FP32 still has to pass it on its own series.
- M2 per-atom virial. The reference and the pair-attention walks store xx, yy, zz, xy, xz, yz on each atom. `dump_virial 1` writes that tensor on extended-XYZ frames. The device dump writes the same six components when `dump_virial` is set. On a 125-atom lattice both the atom-neighbor path and the cluster-tile path match the CPU reference to `2.2e-16`.
- M0 repository skeleton: CMake CUDA project, CPU and GPU smoke tests, CI, and benchmark environment recorder.
- Key-value input parser for the Appendix D sketch. An fcc melt gets zero-momentum Gaussian velocities. `tilemd input.in` runs that file on the CPU reference, writes thermo to stdout, and appends extended-XYZ frames when `dump_every` is set. Those frames reload through `Properties`, including the velocity column. `units metal` and `eam` run a funcfl table with the metal-unit velocity Verlet. A 10^5-step copper NVE drift matches the LAMMPS energy slope, `-1.94e-9` eV/atom/ps. A setfl reader matches LAMMPS `eam/alloy` on a four-atom Cu-Ni cell to `1.5e-15` relative force, and 100 NVE steps stay within `6.2e-15` Å. The stage-1 metal input walks the cluster-pair list. A metal `velocity` line draws `k_B T / (m mvv2e)` with each element's mass and removes the mass-weighted momentum. Metal Langevin is BAOAB; zero friction matches Verlet, and zero temperature damps a force-free pair by `e^{-γ t}`. It runs that setfl file with one mass per element. One thousand Cu-Ni steps stay within `1.4e-13` Å of LAMMPS. A 10^5-step energy series on that four-atom gas agrees through 3 ps and then separates.
- M2 double-precision LJ reference, seven fixtures, and a LAMMPS force comparison for `melt_4000`.
- M3 thread-per-atom neighbor list, LJ force, and velocity Verlet on the GPU.
- M3 32k NVE comparison against CPU LAMMPS, plus drift and RDF drivers for the remaining G1 runs.
- M3 RDF on the 32k melt. The L1 distance to LAMMPS g(r) is 0.00147, inside the 0.02 gate.
- M3 32k drift, 10^6 steps of shifted-force LJ. The least-squares slope of the recorded energy per atom is −3.55e-10 per τ for TileMD and +1.52e-10 per τ for LAMMPS. The absolute ratio is 2.33, outside the 2× gate. Both slopes sit inside two standard errors of zero. The TileMD samples were written with six significant digits.
- M4 stable Morton reorder at each neighbor rebuild, with output still in the original atom order.
- M4 neighbor stencil uses the Morton code of the cell. The old linear index read the wrong range. On an RTX 3050 the GPU suite passes: seven fixtures, a bitwise 10 000-step pair, the skin rebuild, the 32k thermo comparison, the cluster-tile kernel, LJ attention within 1e-10, a 64-atom device cluster rebuild, and both EAM kernels within 1e-9 of the lane walk. The fused kernel, the block-size sweep, and the G5 timing are not measured.
- M4 device-side rebuild decision. The step loop no longer copies status back on every step.
- M4 CUDA graph of 50 steps, with a segment snapshot that restores and grows the list on overflow.
- M4 energy and virial only on thermo steps, reduced on device into a ring drained at segment boundaries.
- M4 launch bounds and read-only position loads on the force and neighbor kernels. Block size stays 128.
- M4 conditional graph node around the neighbor rebuild, with the early-exit kernels kept as the fallback.
- M5 CPU cluster-pair list: x–y columns, stable z order, 8-atom clusters, bounding-box pruning.
- M5 CPU tile force on that list, checked against the double-precision oracle.
- M5 GPU tile force: one warp per cluster, selectable beside the atom-neighbor engine. The cluster list is still built on the host.
- M5 tile-efficiency measurement on `melt_4000` and the 32k melt.
- M5 cluster-pair rows are appended in cluster order. The earlier row starts overlapped, and the corrected tile forces match the double-precision oracle on the fixtures. Efficiency is 0.102 at 4k and 0.0916 at 32k.
- M5 column-width sweep. Targets of 64, 128, and 256 atoms per column keep the same inside-pair count and the same forces. None of them reaches the 40–60% efficiency expectation.
- M6 CPU leapfrog integrator. Over 100 steps it matches velocity Verlet to round-off on the double-precision oracle.
- M6 CPU BAOAB Langevin thermostat. The random draw is Philox4x32-10 keyed by seed, step, and atom id. Zero friction matches velocity Verlet. The atom-neighbor device path runs the same splitting: 20 steps on two non-interacting atoms stay within 1.11e-16 of the CPU. Zero friction stays on the Verlet graph. A step whose neighbor list overflows grows that list at the moved positions and recomputes the force before the second kick; three atoms that enter the shell together stay within 9.95e-14. The fused leapfrog does not apply it.
- M6 fused GPU leapfrog step on the cluster-tile path. Pair coordinates come from `pos4`. The host cluster list is rebuilt when the skin displacement is exceeded.
- M6 fused leapfrog launch. On a 125-atom lattice, 100 steps stay within 2.8e-9 of the CPU leapfrog, both when the list is rebuilt every step and when two graphs of 50 steps are launched.
- M6 fused device thermo. On that lattice, steps 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 match the CPU leapfrog to 7.1e-7 in virial, including one captured graph.
- M4 force-kernel block sweep on an RTX 3050. Block 128 is faster than 64 and 256. A 200-step run of the 32k melt reaches 4.47e6 atom-steps/s. G2 and G3 stay unclaimed.
- M4 graph-unroll sweep. Widths 1, 10, 50, and 200 were timed on an RTX 3050. On the 32k melt the medians are 4.53e6, 4.57e6, 4.55e6, and 4.36e6 atom-steps/s. Widths 1 and 200 match bitwise over 200 steps. The default stays 50.
- M5/M6 path rates on an RTX 3050. The fused cluster-tile run is 0.31× the atom-neighbor engine at 32k and 0.29× at 256k, so the speed gates are not met. One forces-only tile launch moved 730 bytes/atom through DRAM.
- M8 LJ attention overhead. On the 32k melt, two medians of twenty kernel launches put the generic kernel 12.7% and 7.8% behind the specialized tile force kernel, outside the 5% gate.
- M6 fused-kernel profile. One `TileForceKernel<1>` launch on the 32k melt moved 30.10 Mbyte, 941 bytes/atom, at 86% of peak SM throughput. The grid is the atom count.
- M6 skin crossing. A 125-atom lattice with velocity 0.4 moves 0.207 in 100 steps, past half the skin, and the fused leapfrog still matches the CPU to 2.2e-9.
- M6 `pos4` coordinate rounding. On `melt_4000` an absolute binary32 shadow moves the force by `5.81e-5` relative to the double-precision oracle, outside the `1e-5` tolerance. One hundred leapfrog steps on the 40-atom box move positions by at most `5.69e-8`.
- M6 cell-local shadow. `pos4.xyz` is the offset from a cell of edge 4 and `pos4.w` is the packed cell. At the magnitude of a box of length 270 the absolute shadow misses the melt force by `1.64e-3`; the cell-local shadow misses by `9.14e-6`. The fused kernel has not been launched.
- M6 fused-step traffic on the 32k melt. Distinct global addresses in one step are 2163 bytes per atom, above the 60–110 byte target, because each kept cluster reloads `pos4`. This is a source count, not an Nsight measurement.
- M4 non-finite abort. The first non-finite atom is recorded, `emergency.extxyz` is written in original-gid order, and the message names the step and that atom. On the atom-neighbor path, two atoms 1e-100 apart abort at step 0 atom 0 and the frame records `shift=none`. The coordinates are non-finite, so the reader does not reload them. The fused path does the same for that pair, both when it rebuilds every step and when one graph is captured, and the per-step loop now checks the flag after each step.
- M4 periodic dump. With `dump_every` set, `Run` ends the segment at the next multiple and appends an extended-XYZ frame in original-gid order. The fused path stores the leapfrog velocity. On a 125-atom lattice, frames 0, 2, and 4 reload and the last frame matches the device state. The comment includes `shift`. `dump_virial 1` adds `virial:R:6` on that frame.
- M6 reference branch. The fused kernel rebuilds the cell-local shadow and uses the same branch function as the host list. A two-atom wrap on those float coordinates matches a rebuilt list with max `|dF|` = 0.
- M6 fused graph. With per-step rebuild off, `graph_unroll` fused kernels are captured on alternating `pos4` buffers. Thermo is chosen from the device step counter, and the cluster list is replaced on the device only when the displacement is past half the skin. A wrapped coordinate is put back on the reference branch before the stored image shift. On the CPU, 1000 such steps rebuild 64 times and stay within `1.34e-11` of rebuilding every step. The device sort key matches the host grid on the CPU, including a wrapped coordinate and a negative zero. The pair walk and the bounding box are the same functions the host list uses. A coordinate at z = −1 in a box of length 10 is stored as 9. A list that does not fit restores the segment and reruns it one step at a time. The column histogram and the column-key gather return when the flag is clear. The column sort is captured in a conditional node; if that node cannot be captured, the sort stays in the graph. The default still rebuilds every step on the host, and this graph has not been launched.
- M8 metal pressure. Temperature on the 108-atom copper cell matches LAMMPS. Pressure uses `N k_B T` with the `3N − 3` temperature, so it sits 8.8 bar above LAMMPS scalar pressure at step 0; the virial part agrees.
- M8 metal input. `tilemd input.in` now evaluates funcfl and setfl forces with the warp-lane schedule. One hundred copper steps stay within `1.32e-14` Å of LAMMPS, and the Cu-Ni input run stays within `6.2e-15` Å.
- M8 device EAM kernel. The spline sample and both pair scalars are shared with the CPU path. A two-pass device kernel compiles for funcfl and setfl and has not been launched.
- M8 warp-lane walk. The host replay of the device lane map now calls the pair-attention potential, including the embedding update between EAM passes. On the 108-atom copper cell the EAM forces match the cluster-list walk to `4.02e-14`, and 100 metal Verlet steps stay within `1.32e-14` Å of LAMMPS. Cu-Ni lane forces match the cluster list exactly, and 1000 steps stay within `1.44e-13` Å.
- Image counters share one wrap. A coordinate of exactly the box length lands on 0 and counts as one crossing. One velocity-Verlet step that crosses below zero sets the image to −1. The reference, the metal runner, and both device integrators call that function.
- M4 tail graph. A segment that is not a multiple of `graph_unroll` launches one captured graph of the remainder instead of one kernel group per leftover step. On the atom-neighbor path, `graph_unroll` 30 over 1000 steps is 33 graphs plus a 10-step tail. The 125-atom lattice stays within 1.21e-7 in position and 6.62e-7 in velocity of the CPU velocity Verlet after moving 2.35.
- M4 captured group length. `graph_unroll` selects how many atom-neighbor steps go into one CUDA graph. The default 50 divides a 1000-step segment evenly; 30 leaves a 10-step tail graph. The fused tile path still launches one step at a time.
- M6 fused thermo. A thermo step reduces potential, virial, and integer-time kinetic energy once per cluster. The final step is recorded by one extra force evaluation. Eight steps with interval 2 record times 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8, matching velocity Verlet on the CPU. The device path has not been launched.
- M7 CPU prototype of the TF32 and 3×TF32 pair score. It is not on the default force path. On the 4000-atom melt the 3×TF32 force error is outside the 1e-5 tolerance.
- M7 compact-cell remeasure of that score. A cell of about 4 atoms lowers the melt force error from 7.9e-5 to 1.9e-5, which is still outside the tolerance.
- M8 CPU pair-attention API. Lennard-Jones matches the double-precision oracle. A two-pass EAM funcfl evaluation matches LAMMPS `pair_style eam` on displaced fcc copper, including 100 steps of metal-unit velocity Verlet.
- M8 device Lennard-Jones attention kernel. One warp walks one i-cluster on the host-built list. A host replay of that lane map matches the oracle on the fixtures. The device launch has not been run.
- M5 cluster-tile GPU test now uses a 12³ box. The previous 8³ box has only two cells and the engine rejects it.
- M8 pair-attention passes on the cluster-pair list. Lennard-Jones matches the oracle, and the copper EAM tile walk matches LAMMPS to `1.6e-13`. One hundred metal-unit steps on that walk stay within `1.4e-14` Å of the LAMMPS trajectory.
